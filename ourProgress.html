Our Progression

At the end of every week, we wrote a short summary of what we achieved in that week. Here below you can find the progression we made per week.


Week 1:

  • In the first week, the week in which we received the Cansat kit, we immediately started testing the material we received. We also tested and programmed the Arduino. We started with our design, one of the most important parts of the whole project: we’ve made our first sketch on paper and brainstormed about the division of parts. For the soldering, our solderer practised on some test parts and wires, in preparation for our real components that needs to be soldered. For our planning and regulation, we have made a letter for the sponsors which will be sent out later in the project, created an Instagram account, began tracking our expenses.

Week 2:

  • To start our second week off, the programmer has tested the BME680 which can measure a lot! Next to that, we’ve worked really hard on the radio (wireless) communication between 2 Arduinos. Next, our Fusion360 license got accepted, which means we can start with the online designing, so we made a little start with that. In order to work with the BME680, it needed to be soldered, which went great first try. This week, we started with tracking our weekly progress and inventoried with compartments we will be adding and how much these approximately cost.

Week 3:

  • In the beginning of week 3 we’re still working on radio communication. Our antenna wasn’t working properly. We focussed on some small technical compartments, like the gyroscope. We brainstormed about, the weight, size, diameter and material of the total Cansat. We sketched our design on a whiteboard, and we are making significant progress with our online design. Plus, we’ve got a 3d printer to our use. And finally, we inventoried in important parts in group form, and we managed to make the radio communication work: We’ve tested it on a small distance (150 meter), with buildings in between.

Weeks 4 and 5:

  • In week 4 and 5 we had Christmas break, so we could not enter the school. Luckily we could do some testing on parts at home, we could work a bit on the 3D design and we could work on our website. The layout of the website is complete, we just need the text and content. In these weeks, we also ordered 2 parts: a motor for the gyroscope and a regulator for the motor.

Week 6:

  • In week 6, our school opened again, so we could go to our workplace again. To start the week off, we soldered 2 individual parts and we soldered the battery, so that it can power the motor and the rest of our components. The motor for the gyroscopic disk is running and is capable of spinning a foam block!! In this week, we also started on the progress report, which needs to be handed in 21 of January.

Week 7:

  • In week 7, the progress report needed to be handed in, so primarily our focus was on writing the progress report. Next to the report, we 3D-printed the first 2 prototypes of the outer shell of our CanSat. We also made a start with the parachute: the calculations are done; the size and shape are cut out of ripstop nylon and the holes for the ropes are sewed onto the parachute. In week 7 we also ordered a new Antenna. This antenna is a bit big, but it will replace the radio.

Week 8:

  • In week 8, we worked on perfecting the design. We looked for a suitable master switch, and we tested all the compartments together, before we put them in the Cansat (probably in week 9). Our latest design is also printed and so far, all parts fit quite well. Week 8 was also a big week for the parachute. We added extra layer of fabric on the edges, for more stability and the parachute is attached to the hook that we will use. This hook only needs to be attached to the Cansat itself and then ‘’project parachute’’ is done.

Week 9:

  • This week, week 9, is the week of the Test Day. However, on Monday, we noticed that our Arduino wasn’t working. So, Monday we worked till midnight to replace the Arduino and Tuesday morning, we continued at 07:30 am to make everything work. Luckily, just as we were about to depart to Delft, everything was functioning. The Test Day went great: we got nice feedback and our communication test went flawless. During the transport, one wire of the BME680 went loose, so back at school soldered that back into place. This week we also did our first drop test with a weight of 324 gram from 6,7 meter. However, the drop height was too low and our Cansat only reached a speed of 10 m/s. In week 11 we will drop it from a flat, that is at least 10 – 11 meter. To finish this week off, we signed a deal with a sponsor, which will create 5 team polos with logo for us, which we will wear at the launch day.

Week 10:

  • We started week 10 off with a message from the local newspaper. They want to do an interview with us. The interview is planned at 20-2-2026. This week we also ordered new heat-shrinking tubes and some new driver modules In the rest of the week, our main priority was writing the final design which needs to be handed in at the 24th of February. At the end of week 10, we have written around 4500 words.

Week 11:

  • In the final week before handing in our Final Design, we have spent most of our time completing the final design report. We have also done our interview with a local newspaper for our outreach program.

Windfalls

During the project, we’ve had some great moments and some bad moments. Our biggest windfall is soldering: soldering is going well all throughout the project. Next to that, we are proud of our team logo, team name and the story behind those.


Setbacks


Off course, every project has some setbacks. The biggest setback is the fact that active stabilisation is needed. But instead of being sad, we immediately started looking for a solution. 3D printing also took a bit more time than expected, but the finished product is worth the time.


We will continue to update this page as long as the competition is still ongoing.
